Entanglement probe

The entanglement probe tells you, in bits, how entangled the region you select is with the rest of the board. The readout calls this S(A : rest). It only looks: it never measures a cell, never changes the board, and does not use up a move.

In the row under the board, pick Region A (or Region B) the way you pick a gate. While it is selected, clicking the board edits that region instead of playing a move. The number on each button is how many cells the region holds, and the rectangle you drag out is drawn in that region's own colour. Choose a gate or Measure again to go back to playing.

Click cells to add or remove them. Drag to draw a rectangle, or shift-click to stretch one from the dashed cell. Dragging out from a cell already in the region rubs that rectangle out. A region can be any shape, and its cells need not touch.

Every entangled pair with one cell inside your region and one outside adds 1 bit. Select both cells of a pair and you get 0: they are entangled with each other, not with anything outside. Two cells that are not entangled at all also read 0 — so a 0 is not by itself proof that you have found a pair.

With two regions switched on, select disjoint regions A and B to also see their mutual information, the information they share: I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) − S(A ∪ B). A pair split between them gives 2 bits, while cells from different pairs give 0. Take care with three cells or more: in a GHZ state two cells share 1 bit this way, yet on their own they are not entangled.

Your regions stay outlined while you go back to gates and measurement, so you can watch the numbers change as you play.
