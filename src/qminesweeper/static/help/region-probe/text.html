Entanglement probe

The entanglement probe tells you, in bits, how entangled the area you select is with the rest of the board. That reading is written S(A : rest). It only looks: it never measures a cell or changes the board, and it does not use up a move.

In the row under the board, pick Region A (or Region B) the way you pick a gate: while it is selected, clicking the board edits that area instead of playing a move. The number on each button is how many cells that area holds, and the rectangle you drag out is drawn in that area's own colour. Choose a gate or Measure again to go back to playing.

Click cells to add or remove them. Drag to draw a rectangle, or shift-click to stretch one from the dashed cell. Dragging out from a cell already in the area rubs that rectangle out. An area can be any shape, and its cells need not touch.

Every Bell pair with one cell inside your area and one outside adds 1 bit. Select both cells of a pair and you get 0: they are entangled with each other, not with the outside. Two cells that are not entangled at all also give 0, so a zero does not by itself prove you have found a pair.

With two areas switched on in Advanced Setup, select disjoint areas A and B to see their mutual information, the information they share: I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) − S(A ∪ B). A Bell pair split between them gives 2 bits; cells from different pairs give 0. Careful with three or more cells: in a GHZ state two cells share 1 bit this way, yet on their own they are not entangled.

Your areas stay outlined while you go back to gates and measurement, so you can watch the numbers change as you play.
